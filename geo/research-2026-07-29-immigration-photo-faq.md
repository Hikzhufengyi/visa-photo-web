# Immigration photo FAQ source check (2026-07-29)

Scope: primary government sources only.  This note deliberately distinguishes a
document's stated scope from a rule that applies to every visa or permanent
residence (PR) route.

## United States: visa-photo recency

**Supported claim:** A U.S. nonimmigrant visa application photo must have been
taken within the last six months, so that it reflects the applicant's current
appearance.

- Source: U.S. Department of State, [Photos](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/photos.html).
- Exact rule on the Department page: “Your photo must have been taken within
  the last 6 months to reflect your current appearance.”
- Scope: this page is in `U.S. Visas > Visa Information Resources`, not merely
  the passport service, and is therefore appropriate support for a U.S. visa
  photo recency statement.
- Date: accessed 2026-07-29. The State Department site returned a Cloudflare
  access challenge to this research environment, so its displayed
  “Last Updated” value could not be independently captured. Re-check the live
  page before publishing a date-sensitive quotation.

## Canada: PR photo requirements are controlled by the application checklist

**Supported claim:** For an Express Entry permanent-residence application,
IRCC says that the online system creates a personalized document checklist and
that a digital identity photo is submitted only if it appears in that checklist.
This is not evidence of a rule for every Canadian PR stream.

- Source: Immigration, Refugees and Citizenship Canada (IRCC), [Apply for
  permanent residence](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/apply-permanent-residence.html).
- Exact wording in its “Upload your documents” section: “The system gives you
  a personalized document checklist once you’ve completed the online form.” It
  then says, “You only need to submit these if we included them in your
  personalized document checklist,” and lists “digital photos to confirm your
  identity.”
- Scope: this is an **Express Entry** PR page. It directly supports the
  checklist boundary for that flow, but cannot determine requirements for all
  other PR programs.
- **Do not claim:** “Canada PR always requires a photo” or “a photo is not
  required for Canada PR” based on the Express Entry page. PR routes and
  application flows differ; the applicant-specific IRCC checklist or the
  relevant program instructions control.
- Date: accessed 2026-07-29. The live page body was retrieved. It is not a
  technical photo-specification page, so do not derive a size, quantity, or
  format rule from it; obtain those from the applicant's live checklist or the
  relevant program instructions.

## United Kingdom: passport-background rule is not a universal visa-upload rule

**Supported claim:** The normal UK visa process requires biometrics including a
photograph, usually captured at a visa application centre. The central GOV.UK
visa guidance does **not** state that applicants must upload a passport-style
image against a white/light background.

- Visa-process source: GOV.UK, [How to apply for a visa to come to the
  UK](https://www.gov.uk/apply-to-come-to-the-uk). It says: “Provide biometrics
  (fingerprints and photograph).” Its identity section says applicants provide
  biometric information “usually by going to a visa application centre run by
  an external company.”
- Concrete route example: GOV.UK, [Visit the UK as a Standard
  Visitor](https://www.gov.uk/standard-visitor/apply-standard-visitor-visa).
  At the appointment an applicant must “have your fingerprints and a photo
  (biometric information) taken.”
- Passport-only comparison source: GOV.UK, [Get a passport
  photo](https://www.gov.uk/photos-for-passports). Its digital-photo rule is:
  “be taken against a plain light-coloured background.” The same page begins,
  “You need a digital photo to apply for a passport online.”
- Conclusion: the light-background statement is supported for a **UK passport
  application**, not for all UK visa applications. Avoid “UK visa photos must
  have a white/light background” unless a particular visa flow explicitly
  asks the user to upload a photo and gives that rule.
- Source dates: the visa overview records `dateModified` 2024-10-31; the
  Standard Visitor page records `dateModified` 2024-10-31; the passport-photo
  page records `dateModified` 2015-03-25. All accessed 2026-07-29.

## Australia: no universal visa permission to reuse a passport photo found

**Supported claim:** Australia has many distinct visa subclasses, and the
Department of Home Affairs makes document/biometric requirements route- and
case-dependent. No primary source located in this check authorizes reuse of an
existing passport photo for *all* Australian visa applications.

- Source: Department of Home Affairs, [Visa
  listing](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing).
  It lists distinct visitor, study/training, family, skilled, humanitarian and
  other visa subclasses.
- Route-level wording in the Department's Visa listing content (for example,
  Visitor visa material) says: “You may need to provide biometrics at an
  Australian Biometric Collection Centre (ABCC).” “May” and the route-level
  material do not support a universal upload/reuse rule.
- **Do not claim:** “Australia lets you reuse your passport photo for a visa,”
  “Australia requires a new photo for every visa,” or a single age limit for
  every Australian visa. Those claims need the relevant subclass's ImmiAccount
  document checklist or an explicit Department photo instruction.
- Passport-photo rules, if found on an Australian Passport Office/DFAT page,
  concern passport issuance and cannot by themselves establish a visa rule.
- Date/access limitation: accessed 2026-07-29. The Home Affairs site was
  reachable for the visa listing, but its targeted help/search pages returned
  404 responses from this environment. No current official visa page with an
  explicit “reuse passport photo” instruction was verified.

## Safe publishing language

- U.S.: “Use a photo taken within the last 6 months.”
- Canada: “Check the document checklist for your PR program to see whether a
  digital identity photo is required.”
- UK: “Your visa process normally includes a biometric photograph. Follow the
  instructions in your application or visa-centre appointment.”
- Australia: “Check the document checklist in ImmiAccount for your visa
  subclass before relying on an existing passport photo.”
